Use tap REC to capture notes or motion. Use held REC to set up the click or edit timing. Use held REC plus SEL to sample the groovebox's audio. Those are three different actions.

Record notes live or one step at a time

Recording workflows

To fix one take, use EDIT + OCT− before doing something else. To fix one held note instead, go to STEPS and use K1 NOTE, K2 VELOCITY, or K3 GATE while holding that step. Held-step details.

Click, count-in and quantize

Hold REC until the red recording page shows; keep holding while making the adjustment. In the browser, latch REC and then unlatch when done. A quick tap still only arms/disarms.

REC page controls
ControlJob
K1 CLICKOFF, ON while playing, or REC only while recording. The first click in a bar is higher. The click isn't in the source for a sample or the master meters.
K2 COUNT-INOFF, 1 BAR or 2 BARS. It only counts in when REC is armed; press PLAY during the count-in to cancel. A note played in its last half beat can land on step 1.
K3 QUANTIZEEvery step, every second step, or every fourth step. At 1/16 RATE these show as 1/16, 1/8 and 1/4. Labels follow the part's rate.
K4 LEVELClick level.
OCT− QUANTIZESnap existing notes or drum hits to the chosen grid. Locks move with their steps. Instrument notes landing together form a chord, subject to the four-note limit.
OCT+ LEGATO (T2–T4)Extend notes to the next one, wrapping round the loop. This changes gate lengths; in a MONO sound enable its LEGATO separately if you don't want tied notes to retrigger.

Sample the groovebox or send it a WAV

  1. Internal resampling: choose T2–T4 and set EDIT → VOICE → TYPE to SAMPLER. Hold REC and press SEL. REC and SEL blink while waiting. Play the sounds you want, or run the beat. Recording starts when it hears audio and stops at roughly 2 seconds or when you press SEL again.
  2. Trim and tune: on EDIT → SAMPLE, use START, END, LOOP and LOOP START. On PITCH, use ROOT for the key that plays the sample at original pitch (C4 to start), TUNE for fine adjustment, DRIVE, and RECORD AT for 44.1 or 22.05 kHz. Pitch changes also change playback speed.
  3. Hardware WAV transfer: stop the FM-1 and connect its USB-MIDI port to the Mac. The source repository includes app/tools/fm1_sample.py. Run the commands from the repository root; replace the track and filename to suit. A send replaces the sample on that track, so use GET first if it matters.
python3 -I app/tools/fm1_sample.py send loop.wav --track 3
python3 -I app/tools/fm1_sample.py send loop.wav --track 3 --rate 22050
python3 -I app/tools/fm1_sample.py get copied-sample.wav --track 3

The Mac tool accepts PCM or floating-point WAV, mixes it to mono, resamples, peak-normalizes by default (use --no-normalize to keep the level), and stores packed 12-bit audio. GET writes a standard 16-bit WAV. Use track 2, 3 or 4. --port selects among connected MIDI ports.

Limits: about 3.3 seconds at 44.1 kHz or 6.6 seconds at 22.05 kHz across all three instrument tracks, not per track. A new sample can be cut to fit; STORED, CUT means part was kept, BANK FULL means there wasn't room. The FM-1 resamples its own mix, including reverb; the delay pauses because it shares recording memory. No live line input, slicing, reverse or ping-pong looping in 0.4.

Export the right thing

The browser panel's Copy session copies a script for simulator troubleshooting. It isn't a project backup or a WAV export. The current browser page has no WAV import, whole-mix bounce or portable project-backup button. Use internal resampling for short sounds there. On the Mac/hardware route, GET exports only that track's sample, not your full arrangement. See what SAVE actually keeps.

Record the master and separate tracks over USB (0.5)

This is an FM-1 hardware feature, not an export button in the browser groovebox. On firmware 0.5, select GhettoMachine FM-1 as the DAW’s audio input device. It exposes ten inputs and two monitoring outputs at 44.1 kHz, 16-bit. The 0.5 build has been tested in the simulator; end-to-end recording from the physical FM-1 has not been verified.

USB input pairs in the DAW
InputsSignal
1–2Stereo master after the master bus and MASTER level.
3–4DRUMS dry stem.
5–6T2 dry stem.
7–8T3 dry stem.
9–10T4 dry stem.
  1. In the DAW, enable those stereo input pairs and make one audio track for each pair you want. Record 1–2 alongside the four stems if you want the finished sound as a reference.
  2. On the FM-1, play your pattern and check the DAW meters before recording. Track LEVEL, PAN, mute, solo and per-track modulation are baked into the stems. Stems have no limiter: if a stem clips, lower that track’s LEVEL, not MASTER.
  3. Record the master when you want the reverb/delay returns, DJ filter, master chain, or master performance effects. These are not in the dry stems. Turn off the FM-1 click if you don’t want it captured on 1–2.

If you see only the stereo master, check the firmware version and which input pairs are enabled in the DAW. If only the USB recording clicks under heavy load, try a less demanding patch or fewer voices and test again. 0.5 does not provide 48 kHz or 24-bit stems, or separate wet returns.
